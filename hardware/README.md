# Printed capsule parts

Two smooth parts, generated by `make_case.py`:

- `capsule_base.stl` — flat on top, so both sensors stay parallel (the app subtracts their readings to cancel the Earth field). Sensor pockets with corner stops (open on all sides, so the wires leave on whichever edge the module has its pins) and an engraved arrow pointing to the index-finger side (sensor 1); a tray for the battery, the multiplexer and the XIAO stacked, with 2.75 mm on each long side for solder joints and bent wires and room for 2 mm foam tape between the layers; an open Voronoi lattice plate with the same cells as the saddle (bars line up when glued; solid rim, solid bands under the tray walls, around the corner stops and the arrows) and a Voronoi relief on the outside of the tray walls. On the wrist-side wall, a holder for the vibration motor board (21 mm wide, upright in two grooves, motor outwards, 2 mm behind it for the solder joints, wires into the tray through a notch at the top of that wall). Inside the tray at the wrist end, room for the slide switch standing upright in the corner against the little-finger-side wall (mounting ears cut off), its lever out through a vertical window in that wall.
- `capsule_saddle.stl` — held under the base by four printed screws (no glue). Its underside follows the convex back of the hand, as an open Voronoi lattice with a solid rim. A channel in its flat face, the full length, carries a 20 mm elastic strap between the saddle and the base: no slots at the ends, so the capsule is 73 mm across the hand.

- `capsule_screws.stl` — four printed screws: 6 mm coarse thread (1.5 mm pitch, 45-degree flanks), cylindrical head with a slot for a flat screwdriver. Print them standing on their heads, 0.12 mm layers, 100% infill. They go in from the skin side through the saddle into threaded bosses of the base; the heads sit flush in counterbores. Plastic screws are non-magnetic, unlike steel ones, which would bend the magnet's field near the sensors. Do not overtighten: snug is enough. If the thread is too tight or too loose, change `THREAD_CLEAR` and reprint.

`capsule_assembly_preview.stl` shows both parts together with the arc underneath; it is for looking at only.

Print both flat side down, no supports (the saddle with its arc up; its channel roof is a ~21 mm bridge, which PLA bridges fine with normal cooling). PLA or PETG, 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 20% infill.

**OrcaSlicer presets** (Elegoo Centauri Carbon, 0.4 mm nozzle) are in `orca/`: File → Import → Import Configs…, pick all three `.json` files. They build on Orca's own Elegoo profiles and change only what is listed:

| Preset | Based on | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| `Telehaptic case @Elegoo CC 0.4` | 0.20mm Standard @Elegoo CC 0.4 nozzle | 3 walls, 20% infill, no supports, 5 mm outer brim |
| `Telehaptic screws @Elegoo CC 0.4` | 0.12mm Fine @Elegoo CC 0.4 nozzle | 4 walls, 100% infill, no supports, 3 mm outer brim |
| `Telehaptic eSUN PLA-Basic` (filament) | eSUN PLA-Basic @System | nozzle 220 °C, bed 55 °C (smooth and textured PEI), as Orca's own eSUN PLA-Basic profile; written out so they show in the file |

Print the base and the saddle with the case preset, then the screws on their own with the screws preset. The brim matters for the base: its plate is an open lattice, so it touches the bed with thin bars only and can come loose without one. Clean the plate before printing.

Before printing:
- **Measure your parts** and put the sizes into `PARTS` (module sizes differ between sellers).
- **Measure your hand** for the saddle: hand relaxed, a straight ruler across the back of the hand where the capsule sits, touching the highest point; the gap under the ruler 40 mm left and right of that point, averaged, goes into `ARCH_DROP_AT_40` (5 mm now is a placeholder).
